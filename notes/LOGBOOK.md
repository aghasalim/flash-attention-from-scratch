# Logbook

Dated entries, in order. Every kernel change that moves a number gets one. Three lines, not an essay.

This is the highest-value file in the repo and the one people don't expect. It's the difference between "I built a Flash Attention kernel" and being able to answer, two months later, *why* `num_stages=3` beat `num_stages=4` on this specific card. It is also — if you're ever asked to talk about this work — the record that makes the story concrete instead of general.

**Format:**

```markdown
## YYYY-MM-DD — <one-line title>
**Tried:** what I changed
**Measured:** the number, before → after
**Concluded:** what it means, or what I still don't understand
```

Write the "Concluded" line even when the answer is "no idea why." Especially then. The unexplained results are the ones you come back to.

## 2026-08-24 — bootstrap: fingerprinted the machine, and it has no CUDA device
**Tried:** wrote `scripts/env.py` to fingerprint whatever hardware it lands on — full NVIDIA path (device props, SM count, shared memory, copy-kernel HBM bandwidth, fp16/bf16/fp32/tf32 matmul) and an Apple path that measures MPS and CPU for real and writes `null` into every NVIDIA-only field. Ran `.venv/bin/python -m scripts.env` twice.
**Measured:** Apple M4, 10 GPU cores, 25.77 GB unified. Copy-kernel bandwidth 92.3 GB/s on MPS, 94.8 GB/s on CPU (1 GiB fp16 buffers, 2 GiB moved/iter, 10 warmup + median of 20). Matmul, FLOPs=2*M*N*K, 10 warmup + median of 20: MPS fp16 2895, bf16 3154, fp32 2370 GFLOP/s at M=N=K=8192; CPU fp32 1687 GFLOP/s at 8192, fp64 444 at 4096, fp16/bf16 3.3 at 1024 (arm64 torch has no fast half GEMM — 8192 there is a five-minute matmul). fp64 does not exist on MPS at all. Run-to-run spread is large and thermal: MPS fp16 came out 3418 GFLOP/s at 09:50 and 2895 at 09:54, and within one run the min-max was 1791–3793.
**Concluded:** MPS and CPU are the only floors I can measure, and they are floors for *this* laptop, not for anything with tensor cores. The CUDA ceiling for this project — HBM bandwidth, tensor-core TFLOP/s, the measured/advertised ratio task 00 asks for — is unknown, because there is no CUDA device here and I will not copy it from the paper: not measured on this hardware (no CUDA device; developed on Apple M4). Triton has no macOS wheel, so it sits in the `[gpu]` extra and tasks 03 and 05–10 are blocked on renting a GPU. Because the run-to-run spread is ~18% on the same matmul, every later benchmark in this repo needs to report a range, not a lone median. Re-run the fingerprint with `python -m scripts.env`; it rewrites HARDWARE.md and hardware.json.

## 2026-08-24 — online softmax: proved it exact, then measured what fp16 accumulators cost
**Tried:** wrote the derivation and the induction proof in `notes/01-online-softmax.md`, and the NumPy reference in `fa/ref/online_softmax.py` shaped exactly like the Triton kernel will be (outer loop over Q blocks = grid axis, inner sequential loop over KV blocks, fp32 `m_i`/`l_i`/`acc`, causal split into skip/dense/diagonal zones). Then ran four experiments: fp64 agreement with scipy, fp32-vs-fp16 accumulators up to N=8192, an adversarial +100/-100 row, and KV block-order invariance. Command: `.venv/bin/python -m fa.ref.online_softmax` (seeded, two consecutive runs byte-identical).
**Measured:** fp64 exactness is not in doubt — online vs `scipy.special.softmax` max err 0.000e+00 over 1000x1024 rows, and `online_attention` vs direct `softmax(QK^T)V` max err 7.216e-16 across N in (133,256,512) x 6 block shapes (including ones that don't divide N) x causal/non-causal. The accumulator experiment (input fp16 in both arms, reference = fp64 softmax of that same fp16 input, so only the accumulator dtype differs) at **N=8192: fp32 acc 4.755e-08 max abs error vs fp16 acc 1.568e-04 — a 3297x gap; on the denominator, |sum(p)-1| is 2.805e-07 vs 2.301e-03, an 8202x gap.** fp16 relative error passes 1.000 from N=1024 up, meaning small probabilities come back as literal 0. fp32 is flat in N at ~2e-07; fp16's denominator error grows monotonically 5.172e-04 (N=128) -> 2.301e-03 (N=8192). Adversarial row: naive softmax returns `nan` at the peak index in both fp32 and fp16 (exp(100) overflows both), online returns 1.0 with 1.384e-87 error. Block-order invariance: 2.220e-16 over 20 random KV permutations. Zero-padding a short block instead of using -inf: 7.033e-02 error and probabilities summing to 0.430484.
**Concluded:** the recurrence is exact, so any error a kernel shows is arithmetic, never algorithm — which makes the fp32-accumulator rule the thing to check first. Task 03 will hit this: if the Triton forward's error jumps three to four orders of magnitude over the fp16 baseline, the accumulator or the exp is running in fp16, and the shape to look for is the 3297x abs / 8202x denominator gap at N=8192. Two traps that are now numbers rather than warnings: the masked/short-block fill value must be -inf and not 0, and max-abs-error alone will tell you fp16 accumulation is fine at long context (it stays flat at ~1.5e-04 because these score rows stay peaked) while the denominator quietly rots — watch |sum(p)-1|.

## 2026-08-24 — correctness harness: the bar is relative, and this is the number it starts at
**Tried:** wrote `fa/ref/fp64.py` (plain float64 attention, full N×N scores, max subtraction spelled out, no SDPA anywhere — SDPA *is* FlashAttention and would hide any bug it shares with the kernel) plus `tests/{conftest,test_correctness,test_adversarial,test_gradients,test_invariants}.py`. The bar is `assert_no_worse_than_naive`: `err_candidate <= 2 × err_naive`, both measured against the same fp64 reference. Every candidate goes through one registry — `fp64`, `naive` and `chunked` (task 01), `local` (the harness's own textbook attention, for the identities that need an explicit `sm_scale`), and `kernel` (task 03, xfail). Everything runs on CPU for determinism.
**Measured:** 492 tests. Default `pytest tests/`: 262 passed, 38 skipped, 192 xfailed in 11.9 s; `pytest tests/ -m "not slow"` 262 passed, 32 deselected in 13.0 s; `pytest tests/ --slow` 278 passed, 6 skipped, 208 xfailed in 42.1 s. **The bar every kernel has to clear** — max |naive − fp64| at N=4096, B=1, H=2, N(0,1) inputs, naive = `fa.ref.naive.naive_attention`, via `python tests/conftest.py`: fp16 4.753e-04 (D=64, non-causal), 9.586e-04 (D=64, causal), worst over D 1.444e-03 (D=16, causal); bf16 2.039e-03 / 1.057e-02 (D=64), worst over D 1.259e-02 (D=128, causal). Worst over the whole sweep (N ≤ 4096, D ∈ {16,32,64,128}): fp16 2.117e-03, bf16 1.259e-02, fp32 2.300e-06 — `TOLERANCES` is 2× those, rounded up. bf16 is 5.9× worse than fp16 on the same inputs, which is why it gets its own entry.
**Concluded:** the harness is provable *before* the kernel exists, and that is the point — the four invariants (permutation, scale, shift, causal containment) and every adversarial case pass today against `fa/ref/fp64.py` and task 01's references, so the tests are known good before anything is measured against them. Causal containment (rows 0..i bit-identical when K/V past column i are replaced) passes exactly, 0.0 difference, for fp64, naive and chunked. Two things the harness turned up on its own: `chunked_attention` rescales its score tile in place and autograd refuses to differentiate it, so the per-tensor gradient bar skips it with that message rather than pretending; and the shift-invariance identity has to be posed in fp32, because smuggling the constant through the score matmul costs mantissa bits — measured 5.9e-03 (fp16) and 6.5e-02 (bf16) at shift=25, an artefact of the test's construction, not of the implementation. Everything touching `fa/triton/` and `fa/ops/` is xfail and stays that way here: not measured on this hardware (no CUDA device; developed on Apple M4).
